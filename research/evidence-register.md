| Category | Representative evidence | Established capability | Implication for Generative Cinema AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screenwriting/interchange | E-01–E-03 | Structured screenplay elements; FDX, TXT/PDF/RTF workflows; open Fountain syntax | Treat source parsing and lossless provenance as core infrastructure, not an LLM-only task. |

## Research handoff

### Approved facts for analysis

1. FDX and Fountain provide two materially different but important structured screenplay inputs (E-01–E-03).
2. Structured model output, image generation, and asynchronous video generation are available through third-party APIs today (E-04–E-08).
3. Provider privacy and retention behavior differs by endpoint and customer eligibility (E-09).
4. Mature production suites and AI-native previsualization tools already cover significant portions of the proposed workflow (E-12–E-14).
5. Human creative control and documentation remain important to U.S. copyrightability analysis (E-11).

### Unresolved questions requiring primary research or user validation

- Which workflow creates the highest willingness to pay: shot planning, storyboard generation, continuity management, pitch-deck generation, or production handoff?
- Which export contracts matter most: native FDX round-trip, PDF, CSV/XLSX shot list, storyboard PDF, AAF/EDL/XML, or production-platform APIs?
- What are the contractual retention, indemnity, output-rights, and training-use terms for every selected media provider at the intended enterprise tier?
- What accuracy thresholds will filmmakers accept for scene segmentation, character/entity extraction, and shot recommendations?
- Do studio customers require single-tenant hosting, customer-managed keys, private networking, regional inference, or an entirely self-hosted model path?

### Research confidence

**Overall:** High for documented formats and advertised capabilities; Medium for strategic differentiation; Low/unknown for customer demand until interviews and comparative workflow tests are completed.

### Legal and procurement caveat

This register is product research, not legal advice. Before commercial licensing, qualified counsel should review screenplay confidentiality, copyright/authorship representations, publicity and likeness rights, provider terms, data-processing agreements, subprocessors, indemnities, and permitted output uses.

# Generative Cinema AI — Preimplementation Red-Team Review

**Review scope:** current repository plus the approved direction: one product usable by individual filmmakers, production teams, film schools, and studios; third-party AI APIs permitted; hosted processing and retention permitted; intended as a licensable enterprise platform; no target date.

**Release posture:** **NO-GO for external or enterprise release.** The repository is an unexecutable concept stub, not an MVP. Implementation may begin only after the Critical controls below are converted into explicit acceptance criteria and architecture decisions.

## Required preimplementation decisions

1. Approve or reject the staged scope in M-02; “yes” is insufficient to distinguish shot plans, images, and video.
2. Name the first design-partner segment and defer segment-specific capabilities for other audiences.
3. Approve the data classification, retention, deletion, training-use, and provider/subprocessor policy.
4. Approve the tenant isolation model and enterprise identity baseline.
5. Approve a measurable cinematic-quality rubric and human review panel.
6. Select release labels and gates: concept → internal prototype → design-partner alpha → private beta → enterprise GA.

## Red-team conclusion

The concept can become a viable enterprise platform, but the present repository supplies neither an AI engine nor a working application. The largest hidden risk is not model selection: it is treating unpublished screenplay content as ordinary text while serving multiple organizations through opaque third-party inference systems. Product scope must be staged, tenant and rights boundaries must be architectural—not policy-only—and creative quality must be evaluated by source-grounded automated checks plus blinded domain-expert judgment. Until the Critical items are closed, all demonstrations should use owned, licensed, public-domain, or synthetic screenplay material and should be labeled prototypes.

## What remains uncertain

- Whether “yes” means an initial shot-plan product or simultaneous shot-plan, storyboard, and video generation.
- Which customer segment will serve as the first design partner.
- Required deployment regions, private-cloud/on-premises options, retention duration, and provider restrictions.
- Which screenplay formats and export formats must be supported first.
- Quantitative thresholds for cinematic usefulness, latency, availability, and cost.
- Applicable contractual and regulatory obligations in the first sales jurisdictions; qualified privacy and IP counsel must validate them.

### Cross-review disposition

The first-pass documents are directionally aligned on human control, staged media generation, provenance, and enterprise safeguards. They are not yet decision-ready for implementation because scope approval is misclassified, release terminology conflicts, evidence IDs are non-unique, initial ingestion and collaboration scope are too broad, and evaluation/deletion protocols remain underspecified. These are planning corrections, not implementation defects.
